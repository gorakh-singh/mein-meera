      - "2 tsp sugar (adjust to taste)"

method:
  - "In a mortar and pestle, roughly crush the ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and peppercorn. Do not make it a fine powder."
  - "In a small saucepan, bring the water and the crushed spices to a vigorous boil. Let it boil for 2-3 minutes until the water is fragrant and slightly darkened."
  - "Add the loose-leaf tea and boil for 1 minute."
  - "Add the milk and sugar. Wait for the mixture to come to a rolling boil. It will rise up rapidly."
  - "Turn the heat down just before it spills over, letting it simmer. Repeat this boiling-rising-simmering process 2 or 3 times to properly aerate and thicken the tea."
  - "The chai is ready when it reaches a deep, sandy tan color. Turn off the heat."
  - "Pour through a fine mesh strainer directly into cups."

Let us establish one thing immediately: there is no such thing as "chai tea." Chai simply means tea. Saying "chai tea" is saying "tea tea," which is redundant, and referring to it as a "chai tea latte" is a culinary crime that we will politely ignore. 

Proper masala chai is not made by steeping a dusty teabag in hot water and splashing cold milk on top. It is an aggressive, tactile process. The spices must be crushed, the water must fiercely boil, and the milk must be subjected to a rolling, bubbling heat until the whole concoction reduces into a thick, deeply spiced, caffeine-heavy elixir. It is the fuel that runs a subcontinent, served in clay cups on train platforms, in tiny glass tumblers at street corners, and in delicate porcelain cups in grandmothers' sitting rooms.

Every household has its own specific ratio of spices, closely guarded and fiercely debated. My version is heavy on the fresh ginger for bite, with plenty of green cardamom for floral sweetness. A single clove and a peppercorn add a subtle background heat that catches pleasantly in the back of your throat. Making this chai is a morning ritual that cannot be rushed; standing by the stove, watching the milk rise and catch it just before it boils over, is the first mindful act of the day.
